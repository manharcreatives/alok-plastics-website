## 0. Two things to settle before anything is generated

### 0.1 "Plastic generation" is not what happens in that room

The first idea was *"drone flies into the industry, into the room where **plastic generation** happens."*

**Alok Plastics does not generate plastic.** Polymerisation happens in a petrochemical plant — a different, enormous, high-hazard facility. Alok Plastics buys finished resin granules (HDPE, PPCP, nylon) and **injection-moulds them into precision parts**.

A B2B buyer spots this in four seconds, and it invites the question *"do you actually make the resin?"* — where the honest answer is no.

The corrected version is a **stronger** ad: *"we take generic granules and turn them into the exact part your machine needs, to your drawing, batch after batch."* A resin plant cannot say it. A trader cannot say it. **Only a moulder can.** It is the one claim competitors cannot copy.

So: **granules in → your part out.** Same drone entrance. Just honest.

### 0.2 There is no verified "world" proof point on the site

`src/content/site.ts` has these in `proof`:
- `20 Cr+` products delivered
- `70%+` repeat customers
- `1998` since
- `Pan Bharat` delivery network
- `Quarter-on-Quarter` production growth

The tagline says *"Crafted in Bharat, made for the world"* — but **that is an ambition, not a data point.** There is no export count, no country list, no export revenue anywhere in the content.

**Get from the client, before generating:** which countries, roughly what share of revenue, and whether they want the film to claim export at all. If they cannot substantiate it, the film's closing beat shows **Pan Bharat only**, and "world" stays in the tagline where it is an aspiration, not a claim. Putting unsourced dots on a map is the fastest way to lose a procurement team's trust.

## 1. The structural problem, and the idea that solves it

You now want two things that fight each other:

- **One continuous cinematic take** (the trend — feels premium, cinematic, one take)
- **A geographic jump** from Chandigarh to all-India to world (physically impossible in one take)

A drone cannot fly from Ram Darbar to Dubai. Something has to give. Options:

| Structure | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Full montage, 8 different locations | Kills the premium feel. Reads as a slideshow. Every industrial site has this |
| Map-only beat (trucks, port, pins) | Generic. "We ship" is table stakes, not a differentiator. Says nothing about *your* quality |
| **The Part as the thread** ✅ | **Adopted** |

### The idea: never cut away from the part

**The film follows one single component, continuously, from raw granule to finished part installed in a working appliance 1,000 km away.**

The audience locks onto one object and follows it. Nothing else changes. The factory, the machines, the truck, the map, the water cooler in a Delhi shop — they are all just different *addresses* the same part travels to.

This is a real cinematic technique — object continuity, the same principle as *"the red ball"* in animated shorts. It is the reason a factory film feels like a **story** instead of a **montage**, and it is what lets a 30-second film hold the whole journey without ever feeling rushed.

It also solves your actual goal. You said you want the client to *"relate and understand process."* Watching your own product tracked end-to-end, in your own customer's world, is the highest-possible form of that — far better than a map with pins.

**The rule that makes it work: the part must be visually identical in every beat.** Same shape, same colour, same material. This is why we need real reference photos before generating anything.

### Why act 3 is "MEANING" and not "LOGISTICS"

Beat 7 does not show a truck on a highway. It shows **a water cooler actually cooling water.**

That choice is deliberate:

- A truck says *"we ship."* Every supplier ships. It is table stakes.
- A working water cooler says *"your part is doing its job, right now, in your customer's shop."* That is the only reason anyone buys a spare part.

And it lands the distribution message **without a single logistics cliché.** The viewer reads "India" from the setting — the shop, the crowd, the light — and we still get the map overlay for the explicit Pan Bharat beat.

### The map does not go in the Veo prompt

**Veo cannot render a recognisable map of India, nor city names, nor text.** Anything you prompt it to draw will be wrong and will look cheap.

So beat 7's network layer is **composited in post**, over the last 1.5s of the pull-back:

- A stylised India outline, drawn in `--metal-gradient` (`tokens.css:48`) — burgundy to rose, matching the logo
- Thin arc lines radiating from Chandigarh, drawn with a soft sweep-in, 400ms stagger
- 3–5 dot markers only. **Do not dot every city** — it reads as fake data
- City labels set in Archivo, 12px, `--rose-pale`, fade in with the arcs
- Opacity ceiling **55%** — the live footage underneath must stay dominant. The map is a punctuation mark, not the subject

If the client cannot substantiate exports, replace the world arc with domestic-only arcs and hold the "world" claim for the tagline.

## 3. Brand theme — locked, from `src/styles/tokens.css`

High-key, warm, bright, morning light. **NOT** the dark moody lens-flare factory that every AI prompt defaults to. That default is the single biggest reason AI factory footage looks fake.

```
PRIMARY      Alok Burgundy   #581C25   ← the ONLY saturated colour
SECONDARY    Metal Grey      #737171   ← machine bodies, frames
DEEP         Burgundy Night  #2E0A0F   ← shadow detail only, never the overall grade
BRIGHT       Burgundy Bright #8A2C38   ← lit edges, highlights
ROSE         Rose            #C35A61   ← speculars and the map arcs only
SURFACE      Soft White      #F8F7F7   ← dominant tone. This is the video's base colour
LIGHT        Light Grey      #F1EEEF / #ECE7E8  ← floor, walls, mist
INK          Warm near-black #1E1115   ← machine shadow cores
```

**Colour target:** ~70% soft white / light grey, ~20% metal grey, ~10% burgundy. If the grade drifts blue, teal, or orange-teal — the AI default — **reject it.**

**Logo metaphor in film:** two burgundy ribbons catching light around a grey-metal core. Beat 4 rhymes with this: burgundy-trimmed machine, metal grey body, one specular highlight travelling across the mould face.

### Composition rule — already enforced by your own code

`src/components/hero/HeroMedia.tsx:389` applies:

```
radial-gradient(120% 90% at 0% 100%, rgba(30,17,21,.55) 0%, rgba(30,17,21,.25) 45%, transparent 75%)
```

A **lower-left-weighted scrim**. The site has already decided the composition:

- **Lower-left 55% × 70% → must stay calm.** Flat floor, soft wall, gentle gradient. This is where the eyebrow, H1, sub, Devanagari tagline and both CTAs sit (`Hero.tsx:237` onward).
- **Right half and upper-right → carry all visual interest.** Machines, robot arm, mould opening, granules.

**Critical:** beat 4's prompt already specifies the machine sits in the right two-thirds. That is not an art note — it is what keeps the H1 readable over video.

## 4. Veo prompt pack

### How this must be generated

Veo 3 / 3.1 outputs **4s, 6s or 8s per generation — max 8s.** No 30s single generation exists.

- Generate every beat at **8s**, then trim to the scripted duration. Trimming gives you rhythm control; generating only 4s gives you none.
- **~7 generations minimum. Expect 4–6 attempts per beat.** Roughly **150 credits per 8s clip** on Flow.
- **Chain the frames:** export beat *n*'s last frame → feed it as beat *n+1*'s first-frame reference. This is what makes seven separate generations read as one continuous take. **Single highest-leverage technique in the production.**
- **Beat 7 → beat 1** gets the same treatment for the loop.
- Or use **Veo 3.1 Frames-to-Video** with the *same image as first and last frame* to force a perfect seam.

### ⚠️ Veo limitations that hit this film specifically

| Limitation | Impact | Workaround |
|---|---|---|
| **Cannot render legible text, logos, or a recognisable map** | Beat 7's map layer | Composite in post. Prompt "no text, no signage, no logos" |
| **Hands and faces unreliable** | Beat 5's gloved inspection, beat 7's hand on the button — the two highest-risk shots | "Clean white cotton gloves, hands partly out of frame". If a hand still fails, cut to the part alone on the conveyor and let the water run in beat 7 without a visible hand |
| **Machinery physics approximate** | Clamp arms may move wrongly; the mould may not part cleanly | **Anchor to a real photo of the client's actual machine.** Image-to-video only, never text-only |
| **Part identity drifts between generations** | **Kills the entire concept** — the thread is the part | This is the hard one. Requires real reference photos, chaining every frame, and accepting that some beats may need to be re-shot as product-only inserts. Shoot the real parts first |
| **Defaults to dark / teal-orange grade** | Violates the brand | STYLE LOCK on every prompt, plus reject-and-regenerate discipline |
| **Background people drift and morph** | Crowded floor looks wrong | "No people in the background, empty floor" — pre-shift, which is also more authentic |

## 5. From 7 generations to a finished film

### Cut plan

1. **Shoot real reference photos first** — the actual parts, the actual machine, the actual hall. Without these, the part's identity will drift and the whole concept collapses.
2. Generate each beat at **8s**. Keep the best of 4–6 attempts.
3. **Chain frames 1→2→3→4→5→6→7**, then 7→1 for the loop.
4. **Trim to the scripted durations.** Straight cuts only — no dissolves. Hard cuts read as one continuous move.
5. Grade to the palette. Reject anything blue, teal or orange-teal.
6. **Composite the map** on beat 7's final 1.5s. Brand text and logo in post, never in a prompt.

### Asset A — the ambient hero loop (10–12s, seamless)

- **Route 1 (best, one render):** loop the **10–12s segment from Beat 4** — the mould closing. Most hypnotic, most on-brand, least text conflict.
- **Route 2 (cleanest):** a dedicated 8s seamless loop of Beat 4 via **Veo 3.1 Frames-to-Video** with the same image as first and last frame.

Boomerang it for a guaranteed seam:

```bash
ffmpeg -i hero-raw.mp4 -filter_complex "[0:v]split[a][b];[b]reverse[r];[a][r]concat=n=2:v=1:a=0[v]" \
  -map "[v]" -c:v libx264 -crf 24 -preset slow -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart \
  public/media/hero.mp4
```

Verify the seam by scrubbing slowly across the join at 0.25× — that is where loop seams hide.

### Deliverable spec

| | Asset A (loop) | Asset B (film) |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 10–12s | 30s |
| Resolution | 1920×1080 | 1920×1080 + 1080×1920 vertical |
| FPS | 30 | 30 |
| Codec | H.264 High @ 8–10 Mbps | H.264 High @ 10–12 Mbps |
| Container | MP4, `+faststart` (moov first) | MP4, `+faststart` |
| Audio | **Stripped** | AAC 128kbps, native Veo audio |
| Size budget | **≤ 2.0 MB** hard | ≤ 8 MB |
| Poster | first keyframe → LCP element | — |

**Why 2MB is a hard cap:** this video sits on the LCP element of the homepage. A 15MB hero video tanks LCP and TBT on Indian mobile networks — the actual audience for a Chandigarh B2B supplier. `docs/hero-video-brief.md` already budgets ≤2.5MB. Hold that line.

If it will not compress under 2MB, **drop the ambient loop entirely** and ship Asset B click-to-play only. A perfect poster frame beats a slow video.

## 9. Honest cost note

A 30-second Veo film is roughly **30–40 generations** (7 beats × 4–6 attempts), plus grading, chaining, map compositing and encoding. Third-party API routes run about **$150–500** for this scope; Flow/Vertex is credit-bounded but similar in generation count.

Still far below the **$8,000–25,000** a production crew would quote for a 4–6 week factory shoot — but not free, and a bad first STYLE LOCK burns the budget fast.

**Get Beat 1 approved before generating the rest.**
